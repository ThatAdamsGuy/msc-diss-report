Tyre and Strategy Factors

    Tyre degradation rate – How quickly the leading car's tyres are wearing.

    Tyre compound performance delta – Time difference between new and old tyres, and between compounds.

    Out-lap tyre warm-up – How quickly the undercutting driver brings new tyres up to optimal temperature.

    In-lap pace of leading car – How fast the driver being undercut can go on worn tyres.

    Tyre compound choice – Whether the undercutting driver is switching to a faster compound.

    Track temperature – Affects how quickly tyres heat up and how they perform.

    Pit window overlap – Whether both drivers are on similar strategies (1-stop vs. 2-stop, etc.).

Driver and Car Factors

    Driver out-lap performance – How aggressively the undercutting driver can push immediately after the pit stop.

    Driver in-lap performance (for rival) – How well the driver being undercut manages their lap before pitting.

    Car's tyre warm-up characteristics – Some cars bring tyres up to temperature quicker than others.

    Car balance and setup – Can affect tyre usage and out-lap pace.

    Fuel load – A lighter car post-pit might be faster, aiding the undercut.

    ERS deployment strategy – Using more energy on the out-lap for a faster time.

Pit Stop Execution

    Pit stop duration – Time taken to complete the stop; a slow stop ruins the undercut.

    Pit entry and exit execution – A smooth entry and exit can save tenths.

    Pit lane traffic – Other cars in the pit lane can cause delays.

    Pit box location – Front vs. rear garages can affect pit lane timing.

    Team communication/timing – Coordinating the pit stop precisely.

Traffic and Track Position

    Traffic after pit stop – Whether the undercutting driver rejoins into clean air or gets held up.

    Blue flag situations – Lapped traffic may move aside, but still slow things slightly.

    Track layout and overtaking difficulty – If it's hard to pass (e.g., Monaco), an undercut is more crucial.

    Timing of rival’s pit stop – A delayed stop by the rival improves the undercut chance.

Race and Track Conditions

    Track evolution – If the track is rapidly improving (e.g., drying), later stoppers may gain more.

    Weather conditions – Rain approaching? Teams may delay stops, impacting undercut viability.

    Virtual Safety Car (VSC)/Safety Car deployment – Can neutralize gains or favor one strategy.

    Wind direction and strength – Affects tyre grip and car balance.

    Track temperature gradient – Cooling or heating may make earlier/later stops better.

    Time of day/sunlight angle – Shaded vs. sunny parts of track affect grip and warm-up.

Strategic and Psychological Factors

    Team’s strategic confidence – Willingness to take the risk of early stop.

    Opponent’s pit wall awareness – If they anticipate the undercut, they may pit to cover.

    Undercut “bluff” potential – Sometimes a team feigns a pit to bait the rival into stopping early.

    Championship/race context – A driver in a tight title fight may avoid risks.

    Teammate position – A teammate ahead may create traffic for the rival.

Statistical and Historical Insights

    Historical data for that track – Previous success/failure of undercuts at that circuit.

    Previous stints in the race – How earlier tyre changes fared.

    Simulated undercut model – Teams use predictive models based on live telemetry.

Technical and Regulatory Constraints

    Pit lane speed limiter reliability – Any issue here could cost time.

    Delta time rules under VSC/SC – Influence pit stop timing legality and effectiveness.

    Tyre allocation limits – A driver might not have optimal tyres remaining.

    Minimum tyre use regulations – Compulsory use of two compounds might force suboptimal strategy.
